# Waylon Stayley

**Gruntry Crunge** — a hybrid of outlaw country and grunge.

- Country songs made dirtier, heavier, and more swampy
- Grunge songs given twang, space, outlaw attitude, and groove
- Sonic median: **The Black Crowes + Lynyrd Skynyrd**
- Key textures: piano/organ + fiddle when it fits

---

## Waylon Stayley – Gruntry Crunge Set List

### 1. Are You Sure Hank Done It This Way (Waylon Jennings)
Slow it down slightly. Thick organ + dirty guitar tone. Chorus gets heavier and more defiant. Fiddle enters on the second verse like a mournful cry.

### 2. Man in the Box (Alice in Chains)
Strip some pure metal edge. Swampy southern groove, B3 organ under the verses, talk-box riff treated more like a slide guitar. Should feel like it could live on a Black Crowes record.

### 3. I’m a Ramblin’ Man (Waylon Jennings)
Amped-up Skynyrd-style dual guitar attack + driving organ. Keep the swagger, make the guitars crunchier and more aggressive.

### 4. Them Bones (Alice in Chains)
Short, nasty southern stomper. Fiddle stabs on the accents + greasy organ underneath. Dangerous and barroom-ready.

### 5. Luckenbach, Texas (Waylon Jennings)
Keep the heart, darken the undertone. Gospel/Black Crowes-style piano instead of pure country piano. Guitars get meaner on the choruses.

### 6. Rooster (Alice in Chains)
Emotional centerpiece. Stretch it out. Mournful fiddle answers the main riff. Churchy organ in the quiet sections. Southern gothic war story energy.

### 7. Good Hearted Woman (Waylon Jennings)
Slow-burn. Heavy, slightly behind-the-beat groove. Organ pads + crying fiddle. Black Crowes ballad meets outlaw sadness.

### 8. No Excuses (Alice in Chains)
Lean into the acoustic DNA. More open and twangy, add piano, big southern-rock lift on the chorus. Fiddle can double the melody in places.

### 9. Waymore’s Blues (Waylon Jennings)
Funky and greasy. Harder groove, swirling organ, dirty guitars. Late-night roadhouse jam that also plays grunge.

### 10. Down in a Hole (Alice in Chains)
Darker and more spacious. Slow, heavy southern crawl instead of pure grunge weight. Piano + sparse fiddle.

### 11. Mammas Don’t Let Your Babies Grow Up to Be Cowboys (Waylon Jennings)
Keep the sing-along, give it teeth. Heavier guitars, organ swells, more dangerous feel. “Cowboys” should feel like actual outlaws.

### 12. Would? (Alice in Chains)
Main riff becomes more swinging and southern. Organ + subtle fiddle weaving through the verses. Keep the emotional weight, add groove.

### 13. Lonesome, On’ry and Mean (Waylon Jennings)
Full Skynyrd mode. Dual guitars, driving rhythm, mean attitude. Organ pushes it over the top.

### 14. Nutshell (Alice in Chains)
Encore / quiet closer. Almost completely stripped. Voice, sparse dirty electric guitar, piano, and a lonely fiddle. Devastating and intimate.

## Arrangement Principles

- **Guitar tone**: Thick, slightly overdriven, with room to breathe (Black Crowes / early Skynyrd territory — not pure metal, not pure country).
- **Organ / Piano**: Almost always present (B3 or Wurlitzer preferred).
- **Fiddle**: Used like a second voice — mournful, aggressive, or answering the guitar. Never just decoration.
- **Groove**: Slightly behind the beat, with swing. Never stiff.
- **Attitude**: Everything should feel like it was recorded in a hot, smoky room at 2 a.m.
